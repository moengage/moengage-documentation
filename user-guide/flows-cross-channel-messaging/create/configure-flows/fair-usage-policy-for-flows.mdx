## Overview

MoEngage Flows run on shared infrastructure — every workspace on a cluster draws from the same underlying processing capacity. Until now, there was no enforced ceiling on how much of that capacity any one workspace could draw on: a sustained spike from one workspace could degrade performance for every other workspace sharing the same cluster, including yours, even when your own usage was well within normal patterns.

The Fair Usage Policy (FUP) is what fixes this. It gives every workspace a guaranteed, predictable share of that infrastructure, sized to its contracted Monthly Active Users (MAU) — so another workspace's spike can't degrade your performance, and yours can't degrade theirs. The result is more consistent, more reliable flow processing for your workspace, regardless of what else is happening on the cluster.

<Info>
Fewer than 3% of workspaces currently exceed their FUP limit. For the large majority of workspaces, this policy has no practical impact on day-to-day usage.
</Info>

MoEngage already governs usage in two other ways — rate limits at the Data API layer, and billing based on Monthly Active Users (MAU). Neither is designed to catch this specific pattern:

| Existing control | What it measures | What it doesn't cover |
|---|---|---|
| Data API rate limits | Individual request rates to the MoEngage API | Sustained high-frequency event triggers firing across multiple flows at once |
| MAU billing | The size of your tracked audience | How intensively those users trigger flow entries within a given hour |
| **Flow Entry FUP** | Rate of users successfully entering flows in a rolling 60-minute window | — this is the gap it closes |

What makes flow entry volume expensive isn't just the number of successful entries — it's everything evaluated behind the scenes. For roughly every 1 successful entry, about 11 trigger events are processed (the other ~10 fail eligibility checks but still consume system resources). Once a user is in a flow, the system runs an average of 20 decision checks — branching logic, wait conditions, message eligibility — before their journey ends. A single high-frequency trigger like App Opened or Product Viewed, configured across multiple flows, can generate far more processing load than its entry count alone suggests.

The FUP sets a limit on the number of users who can successfully enter flows within a rolling 60-minute window, per workspace.

<Info>
The FUP currently applies to **Event-Triggered Flows** only. Each workspace has its own independent limit — multiple workspaces under the same account do not share a limit.
</Info>

## Your FUP Limit

Your FUP limit is based on your workspace's contracted Monthly Active Users (MAU). The limit recalculates automatically each month when your MAU is updated — no manual action is required.

| MAU Bracket | Limit / hr |
|---|---|
| 0–10M MAU | 250,000 |
| 10–20M MAU | 750,000 |
| 20–30M MAU | 1,250,000 |
| 30M+ MAU | 1,750,000 |

If your MAU crosses into a higher bracket, your limit increases automatically. If it drops to a lower bracket, your limit decreases. Limit changes take effect at the start of each billing month.

If you have purchased a limit extension (see [Increasing Your FUP Limit](#increasing-your-fup-limit)), your total effective limit is: MAU-based limit + purchased extension. Extensions are unaffected by monthly bracket changes.

## How the Limit Is Tracked

The FUP uses a rolling 60-minute window — not a fixed clock-hour reset. At any given moment, the system counts all successful flow entries in the last 60 minutes and compares this against your limit.

**What counts as an entry:**

A user is counted as an entry only when they successfully pass all eligibility checks:

- The trigger event fires.
- The user meets the Target Audience criteria for the flow.
- The user has not exceeded the flow's configured entry frequency.

Users who fail any of these checks are not counted against the FUP limit.

Each successful entry counts individually, including repeat entries by the same user. If a user re-qualifies and re-enters a flow, that counts as a new entry.

## Enforcement

Once your workspace reaches its FUP limit within the rolling 60-minute window, new flow entries are blocked immediately. There is no queue, no retry, and no automatic recovery. Queuing entries to the next hour isn't used, because time-sensitive triggers such as abandoned-cart flows are only valuable within 5–10 minutes of the event.

To get ahead of this, set up a [Threshold Alert](#threshold-alert) so your team has advance notice and time to review flow architecture before entries start getting blocked.

### Dropped Entries

Entries dropped once the limit is reached cannot be recovered. A dropped user will only enter the flow again if they re-qualify and re-trigger the entry condition — subject to the flow's re-entry settings.

## Alerts

All FUP alerts are managed in **Settings > Alert Management**. This page has two tabs:

- **System Alerts** — FUP system alerts are auto-added here by default.
- **All Alerts** — A combined view of system alerts and custom alerts in your workspace.

For a general overview of how Alert Management works, including alert types, subscriptions, and managing alerts, refer to [Overview of Alert Management](/user-guide/settings/reports-and-alerts/alert-management/getting-started/overview-alert-management).

### Threshold Alert

This is a custom alert that notifies your team when usage crosses a threshold you define. You must create it — it is not added by default.

To create this alert, click **Create Alert** in the FUP Status Hub. Alert Management opens in a new tab with **Create Alert on** pre-filled as **FUP for event triggered flows**.

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Default threshold | 75% of FUP limit |
| Default frequency | Once per day |
| Frequency range | Every 1 hour to every 10 days |
| Recipients | Up to 50 email addresses |

Each alert email includes:

- The number of times usage crossed your configured threshold during the window.
- The top 5 flows by consumption, each linked to the respective flow page.
- Suppressed entries per flow during the same window.
- A direct link to the FUP Status Hub.

### Limit Reached Alert (100%)

This system alert is automatically added to your workspace — no setup required. It fires the moment your workspace reaches 100% of its FUP limit and confirms that new flow entries are now being blocked.

This alert does not include flow-level diagnostic detail. Use the Threshold Alert or the FUP Status Hub for per-flow detail.

| Role | Can View | Can Edit | Subscribe Yourself | Subscribe Others |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All users | Yes | No | Yes | No |
| Admins | Yes | Yes (subscriber list only) | Yes | Yes |

## FUP Status Hub

The FUP Status Hub gives you live and historical visibility into your workspace's FUP consumption. You can access it directly from any FUP alert email.

The hub shows the following:

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Rolling 60-min consumption | Live count of entries processed in the current rolling window, compared against your FUP limit. |
| Per-flow consumption | Entry volume and suppressed entry count per flow for the current or a selected time window. |
| Forward projection | Estimated entries that will become available as the rolling 60-minute window advances — shows when capacity recovers. |
| Historical trend | Chart of entries processed and entries suppressed over up to 60 days. The FUP limit line is overlaid and reflects monthly MAU bracket changes. You can filter for shorter time ranges. |
| Create Alert button | Opens Alert Management with the FUP alert type pre-filled. |

## Increasing Your FUP Limit

If your workspace consistently operates near or above its limit, you can purchase a temporary or permanent limit increase in increments of 100,000 entries/hr.

To request an increase, contact your Customer Success Manager and specify:

- The number of 100,000 entries/hr increments needed.
- The start date.
- The intended duration, or indicate that you need a permanent increase.

Your limit is updated in the system before the agreed start date.

Purchased extensions are additive — your total effective limit is always: MAU-based limit + purchased extension. If your MAU moves to a different bracket while an extension is active, the base limit updates to the new bracket and the extension remains unchanged.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Which flows does the FUP apply to?">
The FUP currently applies to Event-Triggered Flows only. One-time and Periodic Flows are not in scope.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is the FUP limit shared across multiple workspaces in an account?">
No. Each workspace has its own independent FUP limit. Multiple workspaces under the same account do not share or pool their limits.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="If the same user enters the same flow multiple times, does each entry count?">
Yes. Each successful entry counts individually, including repeat entries by the same user.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can dropped entries be recovered?">
No. Dropped entries cannot be recovered automatically. A user will only enter the flow again if they re-qualify and re-trigger the entry condition, subject to the flow's re-entry settings.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens to my FUP limit when my MAU changes?">
Your FUP base limit recalculates automatically at the start of each billing month based on your updated MAU. If your MAU crosses into a higher bracket, your limit increases. If it drops to a lower bracket, your limit decreases. If you have a purchased extension, it is unaffected by bracket changes — your total limit is always the base limit plus the extension.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens to the FUP limit during a MoEngage-side incident?">
If a MoEngage-side infrastructure issue or delay causes a backlog of queued requests, the FUP is temporarily paused during the catch-up window, so that clearing the backlog doesn't count against your limit.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
